## 1. #96 levels: what went wrong in v8

### Measured: level rows share their inputs with Clean rows

Every level row is a copy of a Clean base row with the same transcript (`training/out/v8/items.jsonl`):

| family | rows | same transcript as its Clean base | target equal to Clean's | bases |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| l-polished-fix | 500 | 500 | 0 | d-as-said 500 |
| l-polished-homophone | 164 | 164 | 0 | d-mutated 164 |
| l-polished-same | 746 | 738 | 738 | corrections, cancels, names, fillers |
| l-verbatim-keep | 511 | 508 | 0 | d-cancel 89, d-correction-text 84, d-instead 57, d-correction-m6 46, d-filler-text 34, d-correct-later 29, … |
| l-verbatim-same | 707 | 703 | 703 | d-as-said 448, d-mutated 117, … |

- **The as-said overlap is 100%.** All 500 l-polished-fix transcripts are Clean d-as-said transcripts with a grammar-fixed target. The 500 d-as-said rows come from 88 AS_SAID sentence templates, so Polished and Clean also share sentence shapes, not only exact strings.
- **Try 2 weighted the overlap 3 to 1 against Clean.** These are rows of try 2's train.jsonl whose post-prepass transcript is exactly a d-as-said transcript. They are lower bounds, because the prepass changes some of them.

  | level | rows | target |
  |---|---|---|
  | Clean | 291 | as said |
  | Verbatim | 804 | as said |
  | Polished | 867 | fixed |

  So on identical inputs the model saw "fix the grammar" three times for every one time it saw "keep it, no tag".
- **Corrections, cancels and restatements:**

  | level | rows | behaviour |
  |---|---|---|
  | Clean | 1609 | apply the correction |
  | Verbatim | 870 | keep the cue and both sides |
  | Polished | 1017 | apply the correction |
- **The Verbatim targets themselves are right.** l-verbatim-keep keeps every filler it is given: you know 52/52, basically 52/52, I mean 80/80, like 51/51, actually 68/68. It keeps scratch that 24/24 and never mind 35/35. Overall, 391 of 398 targets keep everything. l-verbatim-same, though, is 58% of the Verbatim rows and teaches "Verbatim = Clean".
- **Verbatim works against the system prompt; Polished doesn't.** `dictationSystem` says "remove filler words … apply spoken self-corrections". Verbatim asks for the opposite, signalled by one tag line. Polished only adds to Clean, which fits the base model's prior.

### How the regressions happened

| case | v7 reply (Clean) | v8 reply (Clean) | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| d-as-said-tense, d-as-said-article | as said | "went / saw", "send me a link … get a chance" | l-polished-fix: same transcripts as Clean d-as-said, fixed, 3:1 in try 2 (1:1 in try 1, still leaked) |
| d-num-group-cue-code | "The door code 123 opens the side entrance." | "The door code is 123. Opens …" | the same Polished grammar leak: "is" added |
| h-d-cancel-0 (both tries) | "Let's ask the landlord first." | keeps "… yellow. Never mind that. …" | l-verbatim-keep on 89 d-cancel bases keeps the cancel and what it cancels. "never mind that" isn't caught by the prepass (spokenCancel runs only at Clean), so the model decides, and Verbatim's answer leaked |
| d-restate-place (try 2 only) | "Put the boxes in the basement." | keeps both sentences | ~250 Verbatim correction rows (×3) keep both sides of a correction. The model carried "keep both" over to a cue-less restatement. d-restate itself has no Verbatim rows: `verbatim_plan` returns None for it |
| d-num-correction-control-small (try 1 only) | keeps "four decks" | dropped "four decks" | fixed in try 2; not traced further |

So the tag is learned weakly. The rows contradict each other on identical inputs, and the tag is the only thing that separates them, so the model averages them, input by input. It leans toward whichever level has the most copies, or toward the base model's prior (fix grammar, drop fillers).

### Recipe change (built: gen.py "#96 v9" section)

Principle: no tagged row's transcript (or its sentence template) appears untagged, and no transcript appears at two levels. Each level teaches its contrast on inputs of its own.

1. **Fresh bases for level rows.** `fresh_bases()` runs every program-built dictation generator again under a seed tag, in two draws (`V9_DRAWS`). About half of each draw repeats a Clean transcript; those are dropped, along with d-as-said and any contaminated base. `check_v9` asserts that no level row's transcript (hesitations stripped, lowercased) matches a Clean row's, and that none appears in two level families.
2. **Polished grammar on its own sentences.** `AS_SAID_V9` holds 78 new (as said, fixed) sentences in v7's AS_SAID style: agreement, don't/was, tense, dropped articles, double negatives and other forms. None of them is an AS_SAID sentence. `as_said_dictation` takes the table and a seed, so v7's d-as-said rows are byte-identical (checked: 500/500 against v8's items).
   - **Change from the plan:** l-polished-fix targets come from that rule table, not from Claude. The rule knows the fixed sentence exactly, so a teacher could only drift from it.
   - l-polished-homophone is drawn fresh from d-mutated, capped at Clean's count of homophone-kept rows.
3. **Weight l-verbatim-keep, shrink l-verbatim-same.**
   - **keep:** every teachable fresh base. Each family is capped at 0.45 × its Clean rows, so after 2 copies Clean still leads (v8: d-cancel had 330 Clean rows against 89 × 3 Verbatim). Fillers are drawn first.
   - **The real teach9 gives 1,664 inputs.** That is fewer than the 1,950 aimed for, because the caps and the fresh supply limit it. 66% of them keep a filler.
   - **same:** 150 (v8 had 707).
4. **Rows and copies (split9):**

   | family | distinct rows | copies in train | exposures |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | l-verbatim-keep | ~1,600 after the teacher (1,664 inputs; dry run kept 24/25) | 2 | ~3,200 |
   | l-verbatim-same | 150 | 2 | 300 |
   | l-polished-same | 500 | 2 | 1,000 |
   | l-polished-fix | 500 | 1 | 500 (= Clean d-as-said's 500) |
   | l-polished-homophone | ~150 | 1 | ~150 (= Clean's homophone keeps) |

   Train is ≈ 15,000 Clean (v7's) + 700 lookalike + ~5,150 tagged ≈ 21,000 rows, 25% of them tagged. Valid keeps one copy of everything.
5. **Balance check.** `split9` prints, per family, Clean train rows against the contrast rows (l-verbatim-keep, l-polished-fix, counting copies). It fails the build if any family's contrast rows outnumber its Clean rows.
6. **Verbatim.** One more try with this recipe. **Owner decision:** if Verbatim misses its bar, ship Clean and Polished only, with a Verbatim press sent as Clean (an app change, after the bench).

## 3. #133 Claude teacher (built: PoptalkCore TeacherBatch, PoptalkDatagen ClaudeTeacher.swift)

**Owner decisions:**
- **Model:** Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`), batched.
- **Scope:** Claude writes every teacher row, replacing E4B.
- **Control:** an E4B-teacher v9 is trained too.

### Which rows Claude writes

| family | v8 | v9 | inputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| e-tone, e-translate, e-rephrase | E4B | Claude | 840 |
| d-teach-runon | E4B | Claude | 400 |
| l-verbatim-keep | E4B (punctuation over rule words) | Claude, same word lock | 1,664 |
| everything else (as-said, corrections, lookalike, l-polished-*, l-*-same) | rule | rule | 0 |

### How the calls work

`PoptalkDatagen pipeline --model claude-cli:claude-opus-5-5 [--cache DIR] [--batch 25] [--jobs 4] [--max-batches N]`

- **Rounds.**
  1. Every row runs through the app's Pipeline with E4B's prompts and guards. These are the prompts the E4B teacher had: `e4bDictationSystem` with `e4bLevelRules`, and `e4bEditSystem`.
  2. The model calls are answered from the cache. A call that isn't cached is a miss.
  3. The misses are answered in batches into the cache, and the rows run again, up to 5 rounds.

  Pipeline, the guards and the output shape (`{label, output, reply, rejectedBy}`) are unchanged, and no Prompts.swift text changes.
- **A batch:**
  - up to 25 calls that share a system prompt and JSON mode;
  - system = the app's system prompt + a batch wrapper (`TeacherBatch.instructions`);
  - input = a JSON array `[{id: "r1", input: <user message>}]`;
  - reply = `{"replies": [{id, reply}]}`.
- **Validation and retries:**
  - **each row:** a reply must be non-blank, and an edit's reply must be a JSON object. An invalid row isn't cached and goes into the next round.
  - **malformed batch:** a reply that doesn't parse is sent once more, then split in two, recursively.
  - **failed call:** a non-zero exit or `is_error` is tried twice more, after 30 s and 120 s, then the run stops. The cache keeps what finished.
- **Cache:** `training/out/claude-cache/<sha256(model, JSON mode, system, user)>.txt`, one reply per call. Reruns cost nothing.
- **CLI flags:** `claude -p --model … --safe-mode --tools "" --strict-mcp-config --no-session-persistence --output-format json --system-prompt …`, from an empty temp directory, with CLAUDECODE unset.
  - `--safe-mode` drops CLAUDE.md, memory, plugins, hooks and MCP, and keeps the login.
  - `--bare` would need an API key.
- **Dry run (done, 2 batch calls):**
  - **sample:** 25 l-verbatim-keep rows and 25 edits;
  - **time and size:** 20 s wall for both calls; ~10k and ~14k input tokens, ~2k output tokens each;
  - **CLI-reported cost:** $0.12–0.16 per call;
  - **shape:** 50/50 replies valid, 0 malformed;
  - **gen.py checks:** `v9_teacher` kept 49/50. One Verbatim reply dropped a doubled "like like", where the rule kept both;
  - **contamination:** contamination.py found nothing in the inputs. One kept output shares a phrase with a bench case, and `taught` drops it, as it does for E4B's.
  - **not covered:** run-on rows, the third system prompt.

## 3b. #140 cursor marker (built: commit b2ce36f; owner-approved Prompts.swift exception, v9 only)

- **Prompt.** For cursor models only, `Prompts.cursorTag` adds one line before the level tag and `<transcript>`: `<cursor before="I talked to " after=""/>`.
  - `before`: the cursor's line up to the cursor, at most 40 characters, starting at a word.
  - `after`: the rest of the line, at most 20 characters, ending at a word.
  - It is taken from the whole field, so a short dictation keeps it when its screen text is dropped (#124).
  - No cursor offset means no marker.
- **Gating.**
  - **How:** `ModelSettings.cursorModels` (empty; v9's adapter id goes in at ship, as for `levelModels`) sets `Pipeline.cursor`. PoptalkBench `--cursor` (results get a `-cursor` suffix) and PoptalkDatagen `--cursor` (render and pipeline) set it for a candidate.
  - **v7 and E4B:** every other model's prompt is byte for byte unchanged. Tests cover v7, E4B, and the fine-tune's unchanged system prompt.
  - **E4B as teacher:** with the marker, E4B also gets `Prompts.e4bCursorRule`, a short line on what the marker means. The app never sends E4B the marker.
  - **Not built:** an adapter_config flag. A list keeps the decision in one place with `levelModels`.
- **Insert layer.**
  - **leadingSpace stays.** Model replies are trimmed, so spacing has to be mechanical. The model decides casing and the closing punctuation; no casing rule is added.
  - **New trailingSpace** beside it: a space after the insert when a letter or digit follows the cursor (fixes "Hello there.Existing text"). It applies to every model, v7 included; it is spacing only.
  - **Agreement test:** `leadingSpaceAndMarkerAgree` checks that leadingSpace's previous character is the last of `before`, and that trailingSpace's next character is the first of `after`.
- **Data.**
  - `v9_cursor` moves a mid-sentence cursor in every older v9 row back to the last sentence end or line start. v7's rows put the cursor anywhere, mid-word included, under capitalized targets.
  - 950 new teacher rows, written by Claude. `cursor_teacher` keeps a reply only with the rule's words, its first character (the casing) and its ending.

    | family | rows | what it teaches |
    |---|---|---|
    | d-cursor-word | 250 | continue after a word: lowercase, "I" and names kept |
    | d-cursor-comma | 200 | continue after a comma: lowercase |
    | d-cursor-sentence | 150 | after a sentence end: capitalize |
    | d-cursor-follows | 200 | mid-sentence with text after: no closing period |
    | d-cursor-before-text | 150 | a new sentence before existing text |
- **Bench.** 7 d-cursor-* cases:
  - after a word, after a comma, keeps "I";
  - new sentence after "." (control), empty field (control);
  - mid-sentence with text after ("on Thursday " with no period), a new sentence before text ("Hello everyone. ").

  v9 is benched with `--levels --cursor`.
- **Replay.** History records no cursor offset, so the replay runs v9 without the marker; the cursor rows are measured only on the bench.
- **Dry run (one cursor batch, one run-on batch):**
  - **shape:** 2 calls, 50/50 replies valid, 0 malformed;
  - **cursor rows:** 24/25 kept. The one drop wrote "$50" for "fifty dollars"; number sentences are now out of the cursor pool;
  - **run-ons:** 21/25 kept. 3 drops wrote digits for spoken numbers, and 1 applied a self-correction the rule had kept;
  - **contamination:** 0;
  - **cost:** $0.16 and $0.24.

### Checks on Claude's outputs (unchanged from E4B's)

- **Run-ons:** `m6_teacher` requires exactly the rule words and sentence punctuation.
- **Edits:** they go through `teacher_target`.
- **Verbatim:** `level_teacher` requires `verbatim_plan`'s words exactly.
- **Contamination:** `taught` drops outputs that share a phrase with a bench or held-out case, then contamination.py runs on items.jsonl, and `gen.py check` runs as well.
- **Standing rules:**
  - Clean is verbatim: Claude writes no Clean target except word-locked run-ons.
  - Openers stay: the word locks keep so/well/okay.
  - Formatting and terms come from rule targets.
  - English only, apart from e-translate's outputs.
  - Invented text only.
  - Prompts.swift is unchanged.
